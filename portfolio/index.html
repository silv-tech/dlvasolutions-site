Case Study 01 / ForgeAI

What if a cold email arrived with their new website already built?

Most cold outreach asks for attention before giving anything. ForgeAI flips that. It finds local businesses that need a better web presence, builds each one a custom demo site with AI, and leads with the finished result.

My role
Solo: product, design, code, deploy
Type
AI automation platform
Core stack
Node.js, Express, Claude API
Status
Live at forgeaiagent.com
The pipeline, from a city name to a booked call
  1. ScoutGoogle Places pulls businesses by city and category
  2. ResearchFinds contact emails and reviews their current web presence
  3. BuildClaude generates a custom demo website for each lead
  4. Reach outA personalized email built around their demo
  5. Follow upAutomated sequences, with opt-outs honored
  6. ReplyAI drafts responses aimed at booking a 15-minute call

The spark

Agencies selling to local businesses lose hours to manual prospecting: searching maps, checking who has a weak website, hunting for emails, then writing messages that still read like templates. Nobody replies to "we build websites."

People do reply when they see their own business looking better than it ever has.

Where it landed

The whole outreach cycle runs from one dashboard. Pick a city and a business type, review what the AI produced, and the system handles sending, follow-ups, reply drafts, and tracking.

Open and click tracking for first emails and follow-ups shows daily trends, so every campaign can be measured, not guessed.

The hard parts

Never send a broken promise

Every email promises a demo site. A guard validates the demo URL before sending, and if it's missing or invalid, that lead is skipped instead of getting a dead link.

Replies are untrusted input

Prospect replies go to the AI as clearly marked raw customer text, with instructions to never follow anything written inside them. That's a basic defense against prompt injection.

Built to run in production

Session auth, rate limiting, hardened HTTP headers, send counters, and an unsubscribe list, all deployed on Railway.

Case Study 02 / DCTE

A neighborhood PC shop, running like a tech company.

Davao Computer Trade-in Express sells new and second-hand parts. The owner needed more than a template store: a system for the parts of the business templates don't cover, like trade-ins, loyalty rewards, and listing hundreds of components.

My role
Solo: design, full-stack build, deploy
Type
Client e-commerce + back office
Core stack
Node.js, Express, Claude Vision
Status
Live at dctestore.com
One system, two sides of the counter
Customer
  1. BrowseCatalog with live stock status
  2. Trade inOnline value estimate for old hardware
  3. OrderCustomer account and ordering
  4. EarnLoyalty points on a personal QR code
Shop staff
  1. SnapUpload a product photo
  2. AI fillsClaude Vision pre-fills the listing for review
  3. ManageOrders, customers, trade-in pricing
  4. ScanVerify a QR code, then credit or redeem points

The challenge

Trade-ins, loyalty, and fast-changing stock are what make a local PC shop competitive, and they're exactly what off-the-shelf store builders handle poorly. The owner needed one place to run all of it, and staff who aren't developers had to be able to use it.

Where it landed

The store is live at dctestore.com, and the shop runs day-to-day online operations from one dashboard, with bulk product import and CSV export built in.

Adding a product, changing trade-in prices, or crediting points takes seconds, and none of it needs a developer.

The hard parts

Points are money

100 points = ₱1, so loyalty QR codes are signed on the server (HMAC) and verified through an authenticated staff endpoint before any points move.

Changes go live instantly

Store settings stream to every open browser through Server-Sent Events. When staff update something, customers see it without refreshing.

AI that assists, not decides

Vision AI drafts each listing from a photo, and staff approve it before it goes live. Uploads are size-limited and validated, and the admin side is protected by JWT, bcrypt, and Helmet.
